-- Does crisis create hardness? No---the reverse --

Across 114 pre-registered cells on the 49-industry universe, every
hardness indicator moves opposite to the crisis-ruggedization
hypothesis (Table [tab:regime], Fig. [fig:traj]): SA reaches
the certified optimum $12$--$1000\times$ faster on crisis windows,
trained QAOA angles concentrate $2$--$3\times$ tighter, and descent
landscapes carry up to $20\times$ fewer local minima. Controls
attribute the mechanism: permuting coupling values (preserving their
histogram) preserves hardness, while destroying cross-dependence
removes it---so hardness lives in the \emph{heterogeneity of the
coupling distribution}, which a crisis destroys as all correlations
converge on the market factor (off-diagonal coefficient of variation
$0.17\!\to\!0.06$). Self-averaging probes show optimum-energy
fluctuations shrinking with size everywhere: the ensembles are
statistically homogeneous, with no spin-glass signature.

-- The concentration--hardness trade-off --

One variable---coupling heterogeneity---controls classical hardness and
fixed-angle transferability with opposite signs. Calm-period DC
ensembles are the only classically hard objects in the family, and
precisely there trained angles sit at the non-concentrating plateau
($\sigma\approx1.0$--$1.5$), excluding the fixed-angle protocol;
where angles concentrate (crises), SA solves the instances in
milliseconds. On matched sub-instances, direct per-instance QAOA
shots-to-solution scales as $2^{0.87--1.25n}$ versus SA's
$2^{0.14--0.16n}$: a $5$--$8\times$ exponent deficit
(Fig. [fig:scaling]).

-- A richer universe narrows---but does not close---the gap --

On the 100-portfolio (size$\times$value) universe the instances get
qualitatively harder: convexified certification fails at 600\,s in
13/16 cells, and SA fails to find calm-window pool-100 optima
(best-of-1024 lands $1.1$--$1.7%$ high)---while crisis windows stay
easy, confirming the regime effect at scale. A full-budget
admissibility study found angle concentration genuinely improved
($\sigma_{p2}=0.57$--$0.89$) and the QAOA/SA exponent ratio collapsed
from $5$--$8\times$ to $\sim$$1.3\times$ ($2^{1.01--1.03n}$ vs
$2^{0.74--0.82n}$)---the first trend in the program pointing
toward rather than away from an eventual crossover family. But the
decisive transfer gate failed by $52$--$122\times$: concentration
across same-size instances did not extend across sizes, so no cheap
deployment protocol exists, and per-instance training is not a
protocol. \textbf{Verdict: no QAOA case on financial dependence QUBOs;
the residual hardness is real but classical methods keep a decisive
scaling edge wherever it lives.}
