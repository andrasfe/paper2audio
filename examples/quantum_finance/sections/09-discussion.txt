Three cross-cutting quantities decided every verdict, and none of them
is visible from asymptotics alone. (1) Where hardness lives:
financial dependence ensembles are self-averaging and homogenize under
stress; their hardness, when present, is a collective property of large
coupled pools. (2) Per-operation cost ratios: a quadratic query
advantage is erased by a $10^{8}$ per-call handicap until precision
demands become unphysical---Eq. [eq:criterion] converts this into
a workload screen. (3) Scale overlap: a quantum method validated
on controls is only evidence for a use case if the sizes where the
method is measurable intersect the sizes where the use case is hard;
in case study 3 they do not, yet. These three screens---structural
hardness, cost-ratio, scale overlap---are, we suggest, the minimum a
credible quantum-finance proposal should pass before hardware
experiments are contemplated, and each is measurable classically at
modest cost.
