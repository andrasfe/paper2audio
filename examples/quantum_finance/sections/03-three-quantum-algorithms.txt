-- QAOA --

The Quantum Approximate Optimization Algorithm encodes a quadratic
unconstrained binary optimization problem (QUBO) into a cost
Hamiltonian and alternates $p$ layers of cost-phase and mixer
evolution, controlled by $2p$ angles $(\gamma,\beta)$
 [ref: farhi2014]. Performance depends entirely on the angles; the
practical hope is the fixed-angle protocol  [ref: shaydulin2024]:
on structured problem families, optimal angles concentrate across
instances and sizes, so they can be trained cheaply on small instances
and deployed on large ones without per-instance optimization. Whether
angles concentrate is measurable classically---the circular standard
deviation $\sigma$ of trained angles across sub-instances---making
transferability a screenable property  [ref: qbrainfs2026].

-- Quantum amplitude estimation --

QAE estimates an expectation encoded in a quantum amplitude with error
$\eps$ using $O(1/\eps)$ oracle calls, versus $O(1/\eps^2)$ samples for
classical Monte Carlo---an unconditional, provably optimal quadratic
speedup  [ref: woerner2019,grinko2021]. Iterative variants (IQAE) avoid
phase-estimation overhead. The oracle for a risk workload comprises
state loaders for the market distribution, fixed-point payoff
arithmetic, and a comparator; every component has countable
fault-tolerant cost, so end-to-end feasibility is a resource-estimation
exercise  [ref: chakrabarti2021,babbush2021].

-- Quantum-enhanced MCMC --

Rather than preparing solutions directly, this family uses a quantum
circuit as the proposal distribution inside a classical
Metropolis--Hastings loop  [ref: layden2023]: from state $|x\rangle$,
evolve under a mixed problem/mixer Hamiltonian, measure to obtain the
proposal $y$, and accept classically. The proposal is symmetric, so
detailed balance---and therefore correctness---holds regardless of
noise; hardware imperfection degrades only the speed. Measured hardware
evidence exists for a polynomial mixing-time advantage on rugged
(spin-glass) energy landscapes at low temperature, precisely the regime
where classical local-move chains freeze.
