-- Design, and a gate that worked --

For instance sizes $n\le12$ the full $2^{n}\times2^{n}$ transition
matrix of each chain is computed exactly: quantum proposal
(averaged $|\langle y|e^{-iHt}|x\rangle|^{2}$ over randomized
$(\gamma,t)$), single-flip, uniform, and random-$k$-flip proposals,
identical Metropolis acceptance, three temperatures, absolute spectral
gaps by dense diagonalization; detailed balance verified to
$10^{-15}$. The first version of this study \emph{failed its own
positive-control gate}: an instance-adaptive temperature grew with
$\sqrt{n}$, corrupting slope comparisons. The pre-registered response
---documented failure, one amendment (intensive temperature
$T_k = 2^{-k}J_{\rm rms}$), full re-run---is reported as part of the
result, since it is the controls discipline working as intended.

On the control ensemble the Nature-paper effect reproduces decisively
(Fig. [fig:qmcmc], Table [tab:qmcmc]): as temperature falls the
single-flip exponent explodes ($0.67\!\to\!1.21\!\to\!2.24$) while the
quantum exponent stays flat ($\approx0.3$)---a $\sim$$7\times$
advantage and the strongest quantum-favorable measurement in this
program. On the finance ensemble the frozen verdict is \emph{no
evidence}: the instances at $n\le12$ are simply not glassy (single-flip
mixes with exponents $0.03$--$0.22$), so there is no bottleneck to
relieve. The explanation ties the program together: FF100 calm-window
hardness is a property of the full coupled pool ($n\ge32$) and
is not inherited by small sub-instances---while exact spectral gaps
cannot go beyond $n\approx12$--$14$. The validated method and the
validated hard instances do not yet overlap in scale; sampled
mixing-time estimation at $n=16$--$32$ is the identified bridging
experiment. Per the pre-registered honesty clause, exponents count
chain steps: a quantum step is a circuit (microseconds at best) versus
$O(n)$ classical flops (nanoseconds), so even the control result is
evidence about future advantage on cold rugged landscapes, not a
present-day wall-clock claim.
