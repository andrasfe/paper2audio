This is the frontier chart that decides case study two, and it is read like a race between two cost curves. The horizontal axis is the target accuracy of the risk estimate, in basis points, getting finer as you move right; the vertical axis is wall clock time on a logarithmic scale spanning from milliseconds to hours. The shaded band is the quantum amplitude estimation cost, a band rather than a line because it spans hardware corners from optimistic to conservative fault tolerant machines. The lower line is the measured classical Monte Carlo cost on the identical model, shown per calibration window. Two things to see. First, the vertical gap: at every accuracy a risk desk would ever use, the classical line sits about five orders of magnitude below the quantum band, seconds versus fractions of an hour. Second, the slopes: the classical line is twice as steep, cost growing with one over accuracy squared versus one over accuracy for quantum, so the curves do converge and eventually cross, but only near an accuracy of ten to the minus nine, five orders of magnitude finer than any economically meaningful target.
