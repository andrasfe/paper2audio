This is the scaling figure, two panels, and both axes matter. In each panel the horizontal axis is instance size, the number of assets, and the vertical axis is solving cost on a logarithmic scale, so a straight line means exponential cost and the slope of the line is the scaling exponent. Small dots show individual random seeds, dashes show medians, and straight lines show the least squares fits. The left panel tracks simulated annealing across the problem families: the French one hundred calm family bends upward beyond about forty assets, genuine acceleration, while the steep looking crisis fit is flagged as a measurement artifact, since near degenerate instances saturate the exact hit criterion while their absolute difficulty stays tiny. The right panel is the head to head on matched calm instances: the Q A O A line climbs with a slope of about one, doubling cost with every added asset, while the simulated annealing line climbs with a slope near zero point eight. That is the narrowest gap in the whole program, about one point three times, but Q A O A is still on the losing side, and it has no working angle transfer protocol.
