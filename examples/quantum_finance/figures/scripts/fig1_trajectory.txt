This figure is the rolling movie version of the crisis result, four panels in a two by two grid. The left column covers two thousand five through twenty ten, the right column covers twenty eighteen through twenty twenty one, with calendar time running along the bottom of each. The top row plots the angle concentration of trained Q A O A angles for each overlapping half year window, blue circles for distance correlation, red for mutual information, with a dotted gray reference line at one point four marking the useless, non concentrating plateau. The bottom row plots two market gauges for the same windows: annualized market volatility as a black line, and the average pairwise correlation between industries as an orange line on the right hand scale. The punchline is the mirror image: exactly where the black and orange lines spike, late two thousand eight and March twenty twenty, the angle spread in the panel above collapses downward. Concentration tightens precisely when markets panic, which is the visual proof that crises simplify these problems rather than hardening them.
