Now, case study one: portfolio selection with the quantum approximate optimization algorithm. This is the longest chapter of the talk, and I am going to take you all the way down: how the optimization problems are actually built from market data, number by number, how the classical solvers are implemented, how the quantum screen works mathematically, and what every measurement showed. Take a breath; here we go.

Start with the financial task. You must pick twelve assets out of a pool of thirty two that jointly track the overall market while being minimally redundant with each other. Index tracking funds do this, hedge desks do this, and machine learning people do the same thing when selecting features for a model. The paper encodes it as a QUBO, and here is the exact construction. There are two ingredients, both computed from one hundred twenty six days of returns. The first is a redundancy matrix, call it R, whose entry in row i and column j measures the statistical dependence between asset i and asset j. The second is a relevance vector, whose entry i measures the dependence between asset i and the target, which is the equal weighted market return over the same window.

Two dependence measures are used, giving two problem variants. The first is distance correlation. Ordinary correlation only sees straight line relationships; distance correlation is a more powerful statistic that is zero if and only if two variables are fully independent, so it also catches nonlinear dependence, like two assets that move together only in tail events. Computing it naively costs order n squared operations per pair of series, so the implementation uses the mergesort based estimator, which brings each pair down to n log n and makes the full thirty two by thirty two matrix cheap. The second measure is mutual information, from information theory: how much does knowing one asset's return tell you about another's. With only one hundred twenty six observations you cannot estimate a smooth joint density, so the implementation bins each return series into eight equal frequency buckets, builds the eight by eight joint histogram for each pair, and computes the discrete mutual information as the sum over histogram cells of the cell probability times the logarithm of the cell probability divided by the product of its row and column probabilities. For relevance against the continuous market return it uses a nearest neighbor estimator instead, with three neighbors.

From these ingredients the QUBO cost matrix Q is assembled as follows. For the distance correlation variant, the off diagonal entry of Q in position i j is simply the distance correlation between assets i and j, and the diagonal entry i is minus alpha times the relevance of asset i, where alpha is a mixing weight. So switching an asset on earns a reward proportional to its market relevance, and every pair of switched on assets pays a penalty proportional to their mutual redundancy. The mutual information variant is the same shape with one difference: the off diagonal gets multiplied by one minus alpha. Then comes the cardinality constraint. You want exactly twelve assets, but a QUBO must be unconstrained, so the constraint becomes a soft penalty: lambda times the square of, the number of selected assets minus twelve. If you expand that square, it adds lambda to every off diagonal pair, subtracts a multiple of lambda from every diagonal entry, and leaves a constant offset; the penalty folds perfectly into the same matrix form. Finally the matrix is normalized so the largest off diagonal magnitude is one, putting every window and variant on a common scale.

How big should lambda be? Too small, and the solver ignores the constraint and picks too many assets; too large, and the penalty flattens the interesting structure. The implementation calibrates it by doubling: start lambda at two percent of the largest coefficient magnitude, solve the QUBO exactly, count how many assets the optimum selects; if the count is within two of twelve, stop; otherwise double lambda and repeat, up to fourteen doublings. This calibration is part of the frozen construction, applied identically to every window.

Now the algorithm being auditioned. Q A O A prepares all qubits, one per asset switch, in an equal superposition of every possible portfolio, then alternates two operations. The cost layer rotates the quantum phase of each configuration in proportion to its cost, tagging bad portfolios in their phase; the mixer layer stirs amplitude between configurations that differ by one switch flip, converting those phase differences into amplitude differences. Tuned correctly, interference concentrates amplitude on low cost configurations, and measuring returns a near optimal portfolio with usable probability. The tuning knobs are two angles per round, gamma for the cost layer and beta for the mixer; at depth two, four angles total.

Here is the catch that decides everything. Q A O A performance depends entirely on those angles, and finding good angles for a specific instance is itself a hard optimization problem that consumes many runs of the quantum device. If you must pay that tuning cost per instance, the bill usually erases any advantage. The practical hope of the field is the fixed angle protocol: on structured problem families, optimal angles barely vary from instance to instance and even from size to size, so you train once on small, cheap instances and deploy everywhere. Whether angles concentrate is measurable without any quantum computer, and here is exactly how the paper measures it. For each window, sub instances are sampled from the pool, and a depth two circuit is trained on each using exact, noiseless statevector simulation, meaning the quantum state is represented exactly on a classical machine and no sampling noise enters. Across instances, do the trained angles cluster? The statistic is circular standard deviation, because angles wrap around like a clock face and ordinary standard deviation would be wrong. Place each trained angle as a point on the unit circle, average those points as two dimensional vectors, and call the length of the mean vector R bar. If all angles agree, R bar is one; if they scatter uniformly, R bar is zero. Circular sigma is the square root of minus two times the natural logarithm of R bar, which behaves like an ordinary standard deviation for tight clusters and blows up for scatter; values near one radian or more mean the angles are all over the dial. One subtlety was caught and pre registered before any data existed: Q A O A has an exact symmetry, flipping the signs of all gammas and betas together leaves the circuit output unchanged, and beta only matters modulo pi. Without fixing that gauge, one physical angle setting could be counted on opposite sides of the circle and fake a wide spread. So every trained angle set is first mapped to a canonical form, sign of the first gamma non negative, betas reduced modulo pi, before sigma is computed.

The classical infrastructure has two pillars. Pillar one, certification. The objective is x transpose Q x, where x is the vector of zero one switches. Gurobi, the industrial exact solver, has a general nonconvex quadratic mode, but on these dense data derived matrices it ran three hundred seconds at thirty two assets without proving optimality. The fix is an exact eigenvalue shift, and it leans on the identity that for a binary variable, x squared equals x. Symmetrize Q; compute its smallest eigenvalue, call it mu, negative for these matrices; subtract mu times the identity from Q, which lifts every eigenvalue to at least zero and makes the quadratic form convex, a bowl. That changed the objective, but only on the diagonal, and because x squared equals x you compensate exactly by adding mu times the sum of the switches as a linear term. Every binary configuration keeps its original cost, but the continuous relaxation is now convex, branch and bound gets tight bounds at every node, and Gurobi certifies the global optimum at zero optimality gap in about a tenth of a second. Three thousand times faster, with a proof attached. Every hardness number in this study is measured against optima certified this way.

Pillar two, simulated annealing, implemented for fairness. It is fully vectorized: one thousand twenty four independent annealing runs execute simultaneously as matrix operations, across a grid of sweep budgets, twelve, twenty five, fifty, one hundred, two hundred, and four hundred sweeps, each repeated with five random seeds. For each budget the code records the fraction of runs landing exactly on the certified optimum energy, within ten to the minus six. From that hit rate h comes the headline hardness metric, time to solution at ninety nine percent confidence: the number of sweeps, times the ceiling of, the logarithm of zero point zero one divided by the logarithm of one minus h. That second factor is the number of independent restarts needed so the chance that every one misses falls below one percent. The metric is computed at every budget and the minimum is taken, so slow and steady and fast and lucky strategies both get their best shot. This charges for both attempt length and attempt reliability, which is the standard honest way to score annealers.

There is also a landscape probe, beautifully simple: throw four thousand ninety six random starting configurations at the problem and run steepest descent, always flipping the single switch that reduces cost most, until stuck. Round the final energies to nine decimals and count distinct values: that is the number of local minima found. Also record the entropy of the basin size distribution and the probability that a random descent reaches the certified optimum. Rugged landscape: many minima, tiny descent success. Smooth landscape: few minima.

Now the hypothesis the study was actually designed to test, and the numbers. The widely voiced claim was: crisis correlation structure makes these QUBOs rugged and classically hard, which would be exactly the opening a quantum optimizer needs. One hundred fourteen pre registered cells later, every indicator points the other way. Here is the headline table for the distance correlation variant, pools of thirty two choosing twelve. Calm two thousand six: angle sigma one point four seven; time to solution about one point six million sweeps; hit rate at four hundred sweeps, six in ten thousand; three thousand nine hundred seventy local minima; descent reaches the optimum seven times in ten thousand. Calm twenty nineteen: sigma one point zero four; two point four million sweeps; three thousand nine hundred seventy two minima; descent success zero to four decimal places. Now the crises. The two thousand eight window: sigma zero point four eight, a third of calm; one hundred twenty eight thousand sweeps, twelve times easier; hit rate one point four percent. And covid twenty twenty: sigma zero point seven two; two thousand four hundred sweeps, a thousand times easier than calm; sixty percent of individual annealing runs hit the optimum at four hundred sweeps; only eight hundred sixteen local minima; and a random descent finds the optimum twenty percent of the time. The mutual information variant tells a complementary story: it is easy everywhere, five hundred to four thousand sweeps in every window, so under the frozen decision rule the mutual information arm formally confirms stability. The distance correlation arm is formally scored inconclusive, on a technicality worth appreciating: the pre registration anticipated only two outcomes, ruggedization or no difference, and the data delivered a third, a significant reverse effect, crisis sigma sitting more than two and a half bootstrap noise units below calm, on the wrong side for the hypothesis. The honest reading: refuted, with the sign reversed.

The paper's first figure shows this as a movie rather than snapshots. Horizontal axis, calendar time across two eras, two thousand five to twenty ten and twenty eighteen to twenty one; one hundred nine overlapping half year windows, sliding twenty one trading days at a time. Top panels: circular sigma of trained angles, blue for distance correlation, red for mutual information, dotted line at one point four marking the useless plateau. Bottom panels: annualized market volatility in black, average pairwise correlation in orange. The curves are mirror images. Across all one hundred nine windows, the correlation between average pairwise correlation and sigma is minus zero point five two, and between correlation and the logarithm of solving time, minus zero point six seven. The single tightest concentration of the whole first era, sigma zero point four three, lands exactly on the volatility and correlation peak of the two thousand eight crisis.

Why does this happen? The controls give the mechanism, and they are the evidentiary core of this chapter. Control one, the time shuffle: independently shuffle each asset's return series in time, preserving each asset's fat tailed distribution exactly while destroying all cross dependence. Result: calm window hardness collapses; calm two thousand six drops from one point six million sweeps to three thousand. Hardness comes from the dependence structure, not from fat tails. Control two, the coefficient permutation: take the finished QUBO and randomly shuffle which pair gets which coupling value, preserving the histogram of values while destroying the meaningful industry structure. Result: hardness persists, and even increases; calm two thousand six goes to four point seven million sweeps, and even the shuffled covid matrix becomes hard at one point six million. Put the two controls together and the conclusion is sharp: the difficulty lives in the statistical spread of coupling values, not in which industries carry them. That explains the crisis effect quantitatively. In a crash, every asset couples to the market factor, all pairwise dependencies converge, and the coefficient of variation of the off diagonal couplings, the spread divided by the mean, drops from about zero point one six in calm windows to about zero point zero six in crises. A homogeneous coupling histogram means no coupling dominates any other, no deep competing valleys form, and annealing walks straight home. Control three, the block bootstrap, resamples each window in twenty one day blocks to measure estimator noise. Distance correlation solving times replicate to within a tenth of a percent, seven hundred eighty five point two thousand versus seven hundred eighty five point eight thousand sweeps, so those contrasts are robust. Mutual information solving times swing three orders of magnitude across replicates, seven hundred versus four point seven million, because with eight bins on one hundred twenty six days, estimator noise creates near degenerate optima, a golf course landscape where exact hitting is luck. For that reason all annealing based claims are restricted to the distance correlation family, an honesty restriction worth copying.

One more probe: self averaging, the concept that ends up naming the paper's conclusion. For increasing instance sizes, measure the relative fluctuation of optimum density across random sub instances. In every window and every control, the fluctuation ratios shrink with size, mostly between zero point three and one. Nothing anywhere behaves like a spin glass, where fluctuations would persist. Financial dependence ensembles are self averaging, period. Large instances become typical, and typical means benign.

Could raw Q A O A power overcome this on the hard calm ensembles? This is the scaling extension. Sub instances of sizes fourteen through twenty eight, eight seeds per size. For annealing: time to solution at each size, median over seeds, then a least squares line through the base two logarithms, so the slope is the exponent per added asset. For Q A O A: depth two circuits trained directly on each sub instance in exact simulation, scored by shots to solution, one over the probability that measuring the trained state returns the certified optimum. Results: calm two thousand six, annealing scales as two to the power zero point one four times n; Q A O A as two to the power zero point eight seven times n. Calm twenty nineteen: zero point one six versus one point two five. A factor of five to eight in the exponent, and exponents compound: every added asset multiplies Q A O A's cost by about two and annealing's by about one point one. One artifact is flagged honestly: crisis window annealing exponents look steep, around zero point seven, but that is the golf course effect again; crisis sub instances above twenty six assets become so homogeneous they are near degenerate, the annealer stops exact hitting within the sweep grid, and capped values leak into the fit. Absolute crisis hardness stays tiny at every measured size.

Then the study attacks its own conclusion, twice. First, the universe extension to one hundred portfolios, formed by sorting stocks on company size and valuation into a ten by ten grid, with three pre registered questions. Question one, does hardness grow with universe size? Yes, monotonically: calm twenty nineteen goes one point one eight million sweeps at pool thirty two, four point seven two million at pool sixty four, and at pool one hundred annealing fails outright, the best of one thousand twenty four runs landing one point one to one point seven percent above the certified optimum. These are the first cells in the program where annealing genuinely fails to find the answer rather than merely to tie break degeneracies. Interestingly, exact certification also becomes hard, thirteen of sixteen cells timing out at six hundred seconds, but that hardness is universe driven and regime independent; it comes from the strong size and value factor structure loosening the convex relaxation, and it hits crisis windows too. Search hardness, by contrast, stays regime dependent. Question two, does crisis simplification survive at one hundred portfolios? Yes: covid at pool one hundred takes one thousand sweeps with one hundred ninety eight local minima while calm windows resist with about three thousand nine hundred, and the coupling coefficient of variation again separates the regimes, zero point zero six to zero point zero nine in crisis versus zero point one two to zero point one five in calm. Question three, the scaling exponents steepen to zero point one eight to zero point two five, below the pre registered revival threshold, with the honest caveat that the fit points curve upward, so a straight line likely understates the true rate.

Second, the admissibility study, run because the one hundred portfolio screen showed something the forty nine industry universe had forbidden: angle concentration and classical hardness coexisting. The full budget screen confirmed it, sigma zero point eight nine on calm two thousand six and zero point five seven on calm twenty nineteen, both under the pre registered zero point nine gate, on ensembles that are simultaneously the hardest classical objects in the program. For the first time, the trade off looked breakable. But the deployment gate is the one that pays salaries: freeze the median trained angles from small sub instances at sixteen and eighteen assets, deploy them unchanged on larger instances at twenty two assets, and compare shots to solution against per instance direct training. The pre registered bar was a ratio of at most three. Measured: fifty two point six on calm two thousand six, one hundred twenty two point four on calm twenty nineteen. Concentration across instances of the same size simply did not extend across sizes. Without cross size transfer there is no fixed angle protocol, and per instance training at exact simulation cost is not a protocol, it is a subsidy. Meanwhile the very same study produced the program's most quantum interesting scaling numbers: on matched sub instances of this harder family, annealing itself steepens to exponents of zero point seven four to zero point eight two per asset while Q A O A comes in at one point zero one to one point zero three, so the exponent ratio collapses from five to eight down to about one point three. The paper is explicit about both halves of that: it is the first trend in the program pointing toward, rather than away from, an eventual crossover family; and it is still a loss, on every instance, at every size, with no deployment protocol. A final deep run pushed reference instances out to sixty assets, every reference certified or annealer matched, and found calm two thousand six hardness genuinely accelerating, the exponent rising from zero point two one below forty assets to zero point three nine beyond forty four, cost doubling every two and a half added assets, while calm twenty nineteen's rise fell short of its confirmation threshold. Universe level hardness growth is real but window specific.

So the frozen verdict of case study one: no case for Q A O A on financial dependence QUBOs. The crisis opportunity story is not just unsupported, it is backwards: crises destroy the coupling heterogeneity that is the sole identified source of hardness. Where hardness does live, in calm windows and wide universes, classical annealing holds the scaling edge, and the only known cheap deployment protocol fails its transfer gate by a factor of fifty to one hundred twenty. That closes the first case study. The second changes character completely, because there the quantum speedup actually shows up in the measurements.
