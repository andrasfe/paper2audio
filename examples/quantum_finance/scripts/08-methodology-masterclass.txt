Before the first case study, one more foundation, and arguably the most transferable thing in this entire talk: the methodology masterclass. The results you are about to hear are unusually trustworthy for this field, and that trustworthiness was manufactured, deliberately, by specific procedures. If you ever evaluate, fund, or perform computational research, this chapter is the checklist.

Start with the disease being prevented. It is called the garden of forking paths, and it works like this. Any real experiment involves dozens of small choices: which time windows, which problem sizes, which metric, which threshold counts as success, which runs to exclude as outliers. If you make those choices after seeing data, even honestly, even unconsciously, you will drift toward the choices that make results look interesting, because interesting is what you are hoping to see. With twenty free choices and two options each, you are silently searching a million analysis variants for the one that flatters your hypothesis. The literature on quantum advantage claims is, to put it gently, not immune: a pattern of results where the quantum method wins on carefully selected instances, against classical baselines chosen for convenience, evaluated on metrics chosen late, has burned this field repeatedly.

The antidote is pre registration: write down every one of those choices before any result exists, commit the document to the repository so its timestamp is cryptographically tangled with the code history, and then treat it as a contract. Each of the five studies in this program has such a document, and they all share a five part anatomy worth memorizing. Part one, the hypothesis, stated so it can lose: crisis windows produce harder QUBOs than calm windows, is falsifiable; quantum computing shows promise, is not. Part two, the exact measurement plan: which windows, which pool sizes, which estimators, which seeds, which budgets. Part three, the decision rule: a frozen function from numbers to verdicts, decided while blind. For example, case study three's rule: the quantum proposal wins a temperature rung if its slope is at most three quarters of the best classical slope and the separation exceeds the summed standard errors, and it must win two of three rungs. Nobody gets to argue about what the data means after the fact; the arguing was done before. Part four, verification gates: checks that the pipeline itself works, with the rule that if a gate fails, no scientific verdict may be issued at all, because you would be interpreting the output of a broken instrument. Part five, honesty clauses: pre written statements of what the study, even if successful, would not show, like the chain steps versus wall clock distinction in case study three.

Now, reality: pre registrations meet reality, and reality pushes back. The discipline is not never change anything, it is change only in the open, and only before the corresponding results exist. This program logged every amendment, and walking through three of them teaches more than any abstract principle. Amendment one: while setting up the angle concentration screen, before any regime data was processed, the team realized Q A O A's sign flip symmetry could inflate the circular spread statistic, and registered the gauge fixing convention you heard about. Caught after results, that would have been a devastating critique; caught before, it is just good engineering, and the paper trail proves which one it was. Amendment two: the amplitude estimation study's first verification instance was specified with a register layout whose adder ancillas had been miscounted, implying a thirty three qubit, one hundred thirty one gigabyte simulation that could not run. The instance was shrunk twice, to eighteen qubits, each time documented before the verification result existed. The lesson: pre registration does not require omniscience about computational cost; it requires confessing your corrections. Amendment three is the big one, and you will hear it in full in case study three: an entire study, version one, was invalidated by its own positive control gate, diagnosed, amended in exactly two documented ways, and completely re run. The failed version is reported in the findings alongside the successful one. That is the system working, not failing: the gate existed precisely to catch a subtle artifact, it caught one, and the artifact never contaminated a scientific claim.

Next pillar: baselines that cannot be argued with. Every optimization claim in this program is measured against a certified optimum, a solution Gurobi proved globally optimal at zero tolerance gap, via the convexification trick you will hear described in the next chapter. Every estimation claim is measured against replicated, timed, vectorized classical code on the identical discretized model, so there is no gap for a skeptic to slide into with, but a competent classical programmer would have. Where certification itself became impossible, at pool one hundred, the paper says so and downgrades its claims accordingly, from distance to optimum to distance to best known. Notice the asymmetry with common practice: it is depressingly normal in this literature to compare a lovingly tuned quantum heuristic against an untuned classical one. Here the classical side got the tuning, the vectorization, and the proof machinery, and the quantum side got noiseless exact simulation, which is itself a gift, since real hardware could only do worse.

Then, adversarial controls, the experimental physicist's habit imported into computational finance. A correlation, crisis windows are easier, is not a mechanism. The controls are designed as assassinations: each one destroys exactly one candidate explanation while sparing the others. Shuffle time within each asset: if hardness survives, it lived in the marginal distributions; it did not survive, so it does not. Permute the coupling values across pairs: if hardness survives, it lived in the histogram of values, not the network arrangement; it survived, so it does. Bootstrap the window in blocks: if the metric jumps around across resamples, it is estimator noise, and one entire estimator family, mutual information, was disqualified from hardness claims on exactly these grounds. Three cheap computations, and the mechanism claim, hardness lives in coupling heterogeneity, goes from plausible story to demonstrated fact.

A quieter pillar: measurement hygiene. Every long computation in the program writes its results as it goes, one J S O N line per completed cell, keyed by window, variant, size, and seed, so a crashed or restarted job resumes exactly where it stopped, deduplicating against the checkpoint file rather than recomputing or, worse, double counting. Random seeds are fixed and recorded, so any individual number in any table can be regenerated exactly. Results were committed to version control cell by cell as they landed, meaning even the intermediate states of every experiment are archived. Roughly nine hundred checkpointed results across the five studies, every one reproducible from the committed data by a single command. And a number the authors are quietly proud of: total quantum processor time consumed, zero seconds. Every quantum object was either simulated exactly or counted analytically, which is precisely what made certified, noise free, argument proof comparisons possible at this stage of the technology.

Last pillar, and the cultural one: the program treats negative results as products. The prevailing incentive in quantum computing research is to find the setting where the quantum method shines and publish that; settings where it does not shine die in desk drawers, and the field's collective picture inflates. This program pre committed to publishing whatever the frozen rules returned, and what they returned was two structural negatives, one positive control triumph with a null on the target domain, and a criterion equation. If you internalize one meta lesson, make it this: the value of a feasibility study is proportional to how hard it tried to kill its own most exciting conclusion. Keep that lens on; the first case study will give it plenty of exercise, right after one more foundation: the physics of what makes these problems hard at all.
