This diagram draws stage one of the MultiSignal ensemble, the dual feature routing gate, as described in the paper's methodology and Algorithm 1. The horizontal axis is the normalized Euclidean Krum score gap, computed on the full gradients, with the cutoff tau E at 0.2 marked. The vertical axis is the normalized cosine Krum score gap, computed on the importance weighted projected gradients, with two cutoffs marked, tau E at 0.2 and tau C at 0.5. Each gap measures the same thing in its own metric: the daylight between the worst client the method kept and the best client it threw away, divided by the spread of all the scores. A large gap means an easy call; a small gap means the decision was near a coin toss. The plane splits into four regions. Green regions route to classical MultiKrum: outlier, in the upper right, where both gaps are large; magnitude, in the lower right, where the Euclidean gap is large and the cosine gap is not; and clustered, in the upper left, where the cosine gap exceeds tau C while the Euclidean gap is small. The red region, in the lower left, is evasion, where both gaps are low and nothing separates cleanly. That is the only branch that escalates, to the suspicion penalized QUBO plus agreement voting. Note the dividing line on the vertical axis sits at 0.5 on the left half and 0.2 on the right half, because the paper's conditions use tau C for the clustered case and tau E elsewhere. Note also that in the authors' experiments the outlier and clustered regions were never actually triggered; they exist for completeness.
