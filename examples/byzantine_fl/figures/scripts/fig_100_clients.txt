This chart summarizes the paper's Tables 7 and 8: one hundred clients on MNIST, eighty honest and twenty Byzantine, averaged over five rounds, with the QUBO problems solved by a hybrid quantum annealing service. The left panel is detection accuracy, the right panel is F1 score. Each attack has three bars: blue for classical MultiKrum, orange for the Cascaded Dual-QUBO, and green for the MultiSignal ensemble. The dotted line before the last group separates the six individual attacks from their average. Look first at the four groups where all three bars reach the top: clustered, sign flip, Gaussian noise and scale are detected perfectly by all three methods, because the routing gate sends those rounds to classical MultiKrum, which is flawless there. The whole contest is the two middle groups. On Advanced Lie, 80.4 percent classical, 80.0 for the cascade, 85.2 for the ensemble. On Sparse Lie, 72.0, then 80.0, then 95.2, a gain of 23.2 points over the classical baseline. The averages on the right of the left panel are 92.07, 93.33 and 96.73 percent. Now compare with the F1 panel, where the same story is told louder, because F1 ignores the honest majority that every method retains: on Sparse Lie the bars go 30.0, 50.0, 88.0, and the averages are 80.17, 83.33 and 91.83. The cascade's orange bars, barely above blue on average, are the visual record of the honest negative result that motivated the ensemble.
