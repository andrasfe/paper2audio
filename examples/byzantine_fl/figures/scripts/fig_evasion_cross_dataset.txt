This chart summarizes the paper's Table 6, the comparison that isolates the five evasion attacks, the only ones on which classical detection falls below 100 percent. There are three panels, one per dataset: MNIST on the left, CIFAR-10 in the middle, FashionMNIST on the right. Within each panel, five pairs of bars for Advanced Lie, Lie, Sparse Lie, sign flip and shuffle, blue for classical MultiKrum and orange for the QUBO method, on a shared vertical scale of detection accuracy. The panel titles carry the averages: MNIST, a gain of 8.8 percentage points, from 69.95 to 78.76; CIFAR-10, a gain of 10.0 points, from 71.11 to 81.16; FashionMNIST, a gain of 7.6 points, from 70.40 to 77.96. Three things to look for. Advanced Lie, the leftmost pair in each panel, is an orange win everywhere, and the CIFAR-10 version is the largest single gap in the paper at 22.2 points. Shuffle, the rightmost pair, is the one attack whose verdict flips with architecture: classical wins on the two fully connected datasets and loses badly on the convolutional one. And the blue bars hover around 60 to 75 percent everywhere, which, given that 60 percent is the total failure floor, is a reminder of how narrow the classical method's margin is on this family.
