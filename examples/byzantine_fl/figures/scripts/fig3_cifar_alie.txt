This is Figure 3: detection accuracy round by round on CIFAR-10 under the Advanced Lie attack, blue for classical MultiKrum and orange for the QUBO method. This is the widest gap in the paper, and the plot shows it as clearly as the table does. The orange line sits at 100 percent for most of the thirty rounds, catching every attacker, with a handful of dips to 86.67 and one early dip near the start. The blue line thrashes: repeated swings between the 60 percent floor and 100 percent, with most rounds landing at 73.33 or 86.67, and no sign of improvement across the run. The averages are 75.56 percent for classical MultiKrum against 97.78 percent for the QUBO method, a gap of 22.2 percentage points, and in F1 score the gap is even starker, 38.89 against 94.44. The paper attributes the size of this gap to the model: CIFAR-10 uses a convolutional network whose learned spatial filters give a gradient a distinctive directional signature, so the twenty percent of coordinates that Advanced Lie displaces show up loudly in cosine distance while remaining invisible to Euclidean scoring.
