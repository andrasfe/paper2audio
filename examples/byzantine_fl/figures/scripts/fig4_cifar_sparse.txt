This is Figure 4: detection accuracy round by round on CIFAR-10 under the Sparse Lie attack, blue for classical MultiKrum and orange for the QUBO method. This is the counter example that keeps the paper honest, and it is the plot that best demonstrates why round by round traces matter. In the table this attack reads as a near tie, 72.44 percent for classical against 73.33 for the QUBO method. The plot shows that the near tie hides two completely different behaviours. The blue line is perfect, 100 percent, for the first seven rounds, spikes back to 100 at round nine and again at rounds fourteen and fifteen, and otherwise sits at the 60 percent floor, where it stays for the last fifteen rounds of the run. The orange line is a perfectly straight horizontal line at 73.33 percent for all thirty rounds: one of three attackers caught, every single round, with no variance whatsoever. So one method is sometimes flawless and eventually broken, while the other is never good and never changes. Averaging those two behaviours into a single number, and finding them nearly equal, tells you almost nothing about which you would rather deploy.
