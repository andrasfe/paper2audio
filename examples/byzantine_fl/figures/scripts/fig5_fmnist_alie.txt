This is Figure 5: detection accuracy round by round on FashionMNIST under the Advanced Lie attack, blue for classical MultiKrum and orange for the QUBO method. Compare it with Figure 1, the same attack on MNIST, and the point of showing it becomes obvious: the two plots are nearly interchangeable. The same blue sawtooth with no trend, oscillating between the 60 percent floor and 100 percent and spending most rounds at 73.33 or 86.67; the same orange line pinned at 100 percent for most of the run with occasional drops to 86.67 and one early dip. The averages are identical to two decimal places, 81.33 percent classical against 95.11 percent for the QUBO method, exactly the MNIST numbers. FashionMNIST contains clothing photographs rather than handwritten digits and is a harder classification problem, but it uses the same fully connected model. The paper draws the inference directly: the very similar curvature of the MNIST and FashionMNIST diagrams suggests that detection is architecture driven rather than data driven. What determines whether an attack is visible is the shape of the parameter space it has to hide in, not the pictures the model was trained on.
