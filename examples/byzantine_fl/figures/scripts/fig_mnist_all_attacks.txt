This chart summarizes the paper's Table 3: detection accuracy on MNIST at fifteen clients, averaged over thirty rounds, for all thirteen attacks. Each attack has two bars, blue for classical MultiKrum and orange for the QUBO method, and the vertical axis is detection accuracy in percent. Read it left to right and the paper's central claim becomes a picture. On the left and in the middle you see a wall of full height blue bars at 100 percent: Gaussian noise, scale, targeted, clustered, same value, blatant lie, label flip and stealthy, all detected perfectly by the classical method, with orange bars beside them that are visibly shorter, in the sixties, seventies and eighties. Then there are five places where the orange bar is taller, and each is marked with the size of the gain. Sign flip, plus eleven points, where the classical bar sits at exactly 60 percent, the failure floor. Lie, plus fourteen. Advanced Lie, plus fourteen, from 81.33 up to 95.11, the tallest orange bar in the chart. Sparse Lie, plus fourteen. The only evasion attack where classical still wins on this dataset is shuffle, on the far right. The overall shape, one method perfect where the other is mediocre and mediocre where the other fails, is the reason the paper ends up building an ensemble rather than crowning a winner.
